Recovery happens between visits. Until now, so did the guesswork.

A therapist can only watch so many repetitions. Patients are often unsure whether they are doing an exercise correctly, and progress is judged by memory and rough notes.

{{ app_name }} brings a second pair of eyes into every session. It measures each movement as it happens and keeps an honest, comparable record, so decisions about care are based on what actually took place.

A session, from start to finish

Nothing to wear and nothing to install for the patient. If they can stand in front of the camera, they can begin.

1

Add the patient

Record the diagnosis, the affected area, medical history and the exercise plan once. Everything stays with the patient's record.

2

Choose an exercise or a game

Shoulder, elbow, hand grip, hip, knee, ankle, balance, squats and more, or a game that makes the same movements fun.

3

The camera guides the session

The screen shows the patient's movement live, counts repetitions and points out movements that were not done correctly.

4

Review the results

See the session summary, follow progress over the weeks, and download a clear report to share.

Everything a clinician needs to trust the numbers

It sees the movement, not just the result

Instead of asking how the exercise went, you can watch how the body actually moved, in real time and afterwards.

Measures what matters in recovery

How far a joint moves, how accurate and steady the movement is, balance, smoothness, early signs of tiredness, and one overall recovery score.

Progress you can show

Charts turn weeks of sessions into a picture anyone can understand, for the patient, the family or a referring doctor.

Reports ready to hand over

Generate a clean PDF report for any patient in a click, with results and history in one document.

Made for the clinic

It runs on the clinic computer with a standard webcam. No wearable sensors, no special equipment, no complicated setup.

Private by design

Only clinicians approved by the administrator can sign in, and patient records stay behind that sign-in.

Exercise that feels like play

Repetitive exercises are hard to keep up. Game-based sessions turn the same therapeutic movements into something patients want to do again tomorrow, while every step and movement is still measured for the clinician.
